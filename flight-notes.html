ℹ️ Privacy Info

Privacy: AirPlan does not save your flight report on our servers. What you enter stays in this browser on this device and is not uploaded to us. Your drafts are saved locally so you can leave this page and return later, or switch between several in-progress reports using the menu above. Clear Fields empties the active report after confirmation (it stays in your list). Delete Report removes it from your saved list entirely. For a portable copy, use Export To PDF or Send By Email. Third-party services are contacted only when you use an optional feature that needs them to fetch information for your location (for example forecast data for Fetch Weather Report, map tiles for the preview map, or coordinates from Search location / postcode lookup). Your typed report text is not sent to those services.

ALoS Model (Help)

What to record: Enter minutes in Amber and Red in each battery’s fields below. Use ALoS Comments for narrative: ground risk, air risk (including live traffic / ADS-B-style monitoring), mitigations, and how traffic checks influenced decisions.

This summary is for prompting only. Follow your organisation’s authorised operations material; AirPlan does not replace unit-specific SOPs. Many UAS flight log templates also expect occurrences, observations, and issues as a minimum.

Air / Distance And Height (Typical Envelope)

  • Green-style VLOS band: horizontal distance up to about 500 m and height up to about 400 ft.
  • Amber-relevant areas often include: height about 400–600 ft while still within about 500 m horizontally (increased-height VLOS); and horizontal distance about 500 m–1.5 km at up to about 400 ft (short-range tactical BVLOS, with a common split around 1 km between congested and uncongested where your procedures distinguish them).
  • Red / outside envelope (for documentation): time above about 600 ft and/or beyond about 1.5 km, and any ground-risk Red underfoot (describe in comments).

Extended Envelope: Key Justification Principles

If you operated beyond about 500 m horizontally (up to about 1.5 km) or above about 400 ft (up to about 600 ft) within your authorised ALoS / Amber parameters, record a proportionate narrative in ALoS Comments (or Notes). Sound justifications often cover:

  • Operational necessity - the objective could not be achieved within standard parameters.
  • New TOLA comparison - why finding a closer take-off / landing area was impractical or introduced greater risk (unfamiliar ground, crowd proximity, crew position, operational compromise, time pressure).
  • Dynamic risk assessment - explicitly state one was conducted before and through the phase, what it found, and that risk remained acceptable.
  • ALoS model compliance - the extension fell within the Amber Elevated / BVLOS parameters your operational authorisation and procedures allow.
  • Air risk management - confirm ADS-B or equivalent monitoring was active; note nil traffic or how traffic was managed, and that no elevated air risk was identified where that applies.
  • Proportionality - state the extension was the minimum necessary distance or height to achieve the objective.
  • FRZ / NOTAM awareness - confirm no restricted airspace was entered; known restrictions (e.g. an FRZ south of or near the area) were actively avoided.

Ground Risk (Prompts For Comments)

  • Uninvolved persons, assemblies, separations (e.g. 10 m / 30 m where your procedures apply).
  • Roads, schools, sensitive sites, changing foot traffic; dynamic underfoot assessment (e.g. GREZ / density-style red-amber-green).

Air Risk (Prompts For Comments)

  • FRZ proximity, NOTAMs, and other airspace factors (see the Airspace section on this form when you have coordinates).
  • Live traffic awareness: actively use a suitable tool, especially in amber or extended-distance phases; record what you saw and what you did, not only “ADS-B monitored.”

ADS-B / Traffic Monitoring (What To Record)

“ADS-B monitored” should mean you used a live airspace or traffic tool (e.g. Drone Assist, Altitude Angel, AirNav RadarBox, a dedicated ADS-B receiver app, or traffic layers in DJI Fly or Garmin Pilot where applicable) to detect manned aircraft through the flight or at defined phases. It is an air-risk mitigation: document what you checked, when, what it showed, and how that affected your plan.

  • Tool or app used (name it).
  • Who monitored (pilot, observer, or both).
  • Frequency: continuous, or spot-checks at key phases (pre-flight, before extending beyond 500 m, approach/landing, etc.); note times if spot-checks.
  • What was seen: even if nothing, state nil traffic identified rather than leaving it blank.
  • Actions in response (e.g. height reduction, pause, hold), or confirm no change.

Long pre-flight narratives (tool check, NOTAM reference, FRZ intent) can also go in Notes or other mission fields if they do not fit one battery’s ALoS box.

Example Phrasing: Traffic Checks, Distance/Height Templates, Principles Checklist, RAG Stage 3 (Tap To Expand)

Pre-flight (example): Pre-flight airspace check using [app]; no manned traffic identified in operational area; monitoring active on [device]; NOTAM [ref] noted; FRZ to south; intend to remain clear.

During flight (example): Traffic tool maintained through all phases; nil traffic; or: one aircraft transiting [direction] at [altitude]; assessed no conflict; reduced UAS height as precaution; continued.

Standard (example): Monitoring active via [tool] throughout; nil traffic; air risk assessed low for sector duration.

Before extending distance (example): Prior to extending beyond 500 m, traffic check; nil conflict; monitoring continued through extended phase; air risk unchanged from pre-flight.

Justifying Distance Beyond 500 m (Up To 1.5 km)

These are template-style examples you can adapt directly into a flight report:

Example 1 - Operational necessity, objective unachievable within 500 m: During the operation, the primary objective could not be achieved within the standard 500 m VLOS envelope. The subject/target area was located at approximately 700 m from the established take-off/landing point. Repositioning the drone to a closer TOLA would have required identification of a new take-off location, introduction of separate and unassessed ground risks, disruption to the operational team’s position, and potential compromise of the operation. An extension to 700 m within the Amber Elevated Risk parameters was assessed as the safer and more proportionate option. A dynamic risk assessment was conducted prior to and throughout the extension. Air risk was managed via continuous ADS-B monitoring. The UAS remained within tactical BVLOS short-range parameters (under 1 km, congested area). The extension was deemed necessary, proportionate, and the lowest-risk means of achieving the operational objective.

Example 2 - Dynamic reposition during active operation: During active flight, the subject moved beyond the initial 500 m operational boundary to approximately 650 m. Terminating the flight and repositioning to a new TOLA was assessed as impractical due to time constraints and would have introduced new and unassessed ground risks at an unfamiliar launch point. The area beyond 500 m was already known and assessed as part of pre-flight planning (Amber Elevated Risk corridor up to 1 km). A dynamic risk assessment confirmed no change to air risk profile. ADS-B traffic monitoring was maintained throughout. Extension beyond 500 m was brief, controlled, and directly necessary to fulfil the operational objective.

Justifying Height Above 400 ft (Up To 600 ft)

Example 3 - Increased height for operational overview: An increase in altitude above 400 ft (to approximately 520 ft) was required to achieve an effective overview of the operational area. At 400 ft, terrain features/structures obscured the required field of view. The ALoS model applicable to this operation permits increased height within VLOS-only parameters up to 600 ft. The elevated position reduced horizontal separation requirements and allowed the pilot to maintain VLOS throughout. No controlled airspace conflicts were identified. The height increase was the minimum necessary to achieve the objective and was assessed as proportionate to the operational requirement.

Key Justification Principles To Always Include

These elements underpin a sound justification; use them as a checklist:

  • Operational necessity - the objective could not be achieved within standard parameters.
  • New TOLA comparison - explain why finding a closer launch point was impractical or introduced greater risk (unfamiliar ground, crowd proximity, operational compromise, time pressure).
  • Dynamic risk assessment - explicitly state one was conducted, what it found, and that risk remained acceptable.
  • ALoS model compliance - reference that the extension fell within the Amber Elevated / BVLOS parameters authorised under your operational authorisation.
  • Air risk management - confirm ADS-B monitoring was active and no elevated air risk was identified where that applies.
  • Proportionality - state the extension was the minimum necessary distance or height to achieve the objective.
  • FRZ / NOTAM awareness - confirm no restricted airspace was entered and any known restrictions (e.g. the FRZ south of the area) were actively avoided.

Wording For The RAG Stage 3 Dynamic Assessment

Referencing the three-stage RAG model in your documents, you can add:

RAG Stage 3 (example): Stage 3 ongoing dynamic RAG assessment was maintained throughout. The GREZ disc beneath the drone was assessed continuously as it transitioned from the green VLOS zone into the amber extended envelope. The pilot articulated and recorded the transition verbally to the crew. At no point did the dynamic assessment identify a change requiring immediate termination of the extended phase.

Weather

Uses the same Open-Meteo API as Flight Weather. Coordinates come from Location or an Additional Operational Location with a resolved lat/lng. When you have more than one site, choose which to use above. Forecast values use your mission Date and Time when both are set.

How This Is Calculated

When Date or Time is missing, the report uses the latest Open-Meteo hourly bin at or before the current moment (same idea as choosing Now on Flight Weather).

Green / Amber / Red is a forecast indicator against the selected aircraft profile — not a go/no-go decision. Confirm conditions on site and apply your Operations Manual before flying.

Thresholds differ by profile (e.g. Matrice 4 uses 10 m and 120 m wind; Mini 5 Pro uses 10 m only with stricter gust margins). Expand How This Is Calculated on the Flight Weather Summary tab for the full threshold list.
